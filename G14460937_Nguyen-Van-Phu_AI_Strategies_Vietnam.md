## Part 1: Research on Vietnam's AI strategic directions

In September 2019, Resolution No. 52-NQ/TW was issued in the Fourth Industrial Revolution – Industry 4.0, which highlighted AI as a national priority for socio-economic transformation (LNT & PARTNERS, 2024). This resolution alone was not enough to drive changes in practice, which led to the release of Resolution No. 50/NQ-CP to implement previously established directions by assigning specific tasks to relevant ministries and formally requesting to develop a dedicated national AI strategy.

However, Vietnam's situation highlighted the need for immediate action. According to a report on AI in Southeast Asia in 2021, nearly half of Vietnamese organizations had not yet scaled up their AI initiatives, but still remained at the testing stage rather than applying the technology across different sectors (Asia Society, n.d.). This showed that while there was awareness of AI technology, the country still lacked a structured national framework to turn that awareness into large-scale projects and tranformative results.

Beyond this, the projected economic benefits of AI further emphasized the importance of a coordinated national response. Based on a report by Google, AI is expected to contribute up to approximately USD 79.3 billion to Vietnam's economy by 2030, accounted for nearly 12% of the country's GDP (Vietnam News, 2024). Without a clear national direction, Vietnam may risk missing this opportunity and falling behind regional competitors such as Singapore, Malaysia, and Thailand, which had already developed their own AI strategies.

Together, these two factors, including the slow pace of AI adoption and the enormous economic potential, pointed to the urgent need for a more organized approach at the national level.

In response to this, Vietnam officially introduced Decision No. 127/QD-TTg in 2021 as the first national strategy for AI research, developments, and applications, marking the moment when Vietnam's AI ambition moved from political intention to a detailed national roadmap.

This strategy aims to position Vietnam as a leading innovator and hub for AI solutions within ASEAN and globally by 2030, with a particular focus on AI applications in national defence, public administration, and economic development. There are two clear visions in this strategy for both 2025 and 2030 and taken directly from Decision 127:

**By 2025:** Vietnam will be ranked among the **top 5 in ASEAN** and **top 60 in the world** in AI research, development, and application.

**By 2030:** Vietnam will become a **center for AI innovation** and be ranked in the **top 4 in ASEAN** and **top 50 in the world**.

Furthermore, it is guided by one clear principle **"AI should be developed with people and businesses at the center"** to prevent inappropraite use of technology and protect the rights of individuals and organizations. This people-first approach also shows that the Vietnamese government was aware of the social risks that might result from the rapid AI adoption.

Moreover, it is built around five strategic directions, with each being supported by specific programs and projects, with assigned ministries and clear timelines.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Strategic direction 1** | Building a system of legal documents and regulations related to AI |
| **Strategic direction 2** | Building data and computing infrastructure for the research, development and application of AI |
| **Strategic direction 3** | Developing the AI ecosystem |
| **Strategic direction 4** | Promoting the application of AI |
| **Strategic direction 5** | Promote international cooperation in AI |

Among these five strategic directions, this report focuses on Strategies 2, 3, and 4, because they cover the full journey of AI development, from building the infrastructure, developing human resources, to putting AI applications into practice. Strategy 2 provides the technical foundation, Strategy 3 develops human and institutional capacity, and Strategy 4 ensures that these investments are translated into real-world outcomes.

Since this strategy was issued in 2021, Vietnam has made clear progress in its global AI standing. Vietnam was ranked among the top five in ASEAN in the 2024 Government AI Readiness Index and placed third and fiffth globally in AI trust and AI acceptance, respectively (Vietnam Plus, 2025). In addition, the Microsoft Global AI Diffusion Q1 2026 Report revealed that Vietnam recorded a 24.9% increase in AI user share since June 2025 (Microsoft, 2026), which showed that the national strategy is also beginning to produce real results at the population level. Also, the private sector responded positively, as funding for Vietnamese AI companies grew eightfold in just one year (Vietnam Plus, 2025).

Building on this foundation, the following section examines three of the five strategic directions outlined in Decision 127 in greater detail.

## Part 2: Three strategic directions

### Strategic direction 1: Building data and computing infrastructure for AI research, development, application.

Decision 127 identifies data and computing infrastructure as the technical base for the entire national AI effort. This strategy focuses on building a national system of shared and open databases that can be easily accessed for AI research and development purposes, based on the principle that data sharing should benefit all parties involved while still protecting individual privacy. Furthermore, the decision also calls for the development of Vietnam's computing capacity, including high-performance, cloud, and fod computing.

#### Goals

- Establish **1 national center** for big data storage and high-performance, cloud, and fog computing
- Establish **3 national centers** that are integrated into a shared national network for big data and computing for AI.
- Develop **50 open, linked, and connnected data sets** to facilitate the development and use of AI across several economic sectors.

#### Deadline

- 1 national data center: **2025**
- 3 national centers and 50 open datasets: **2030**

#### Significance

The success of other strategic directions largely depends on what this strategy delivers. Without strong data infrastructure, AI models cannot be trained effectively and AI services cannot be delivered at a national scale. This approach is further guided by the principle of mutual benefits, which are encouraging data sharing while ensuring decentralized access and protecting the privacy of individuals and organizations.

---

### Strategic direction 2: Developing the AI ecosystem

Decision 127 outlines four interconnected areas for developing the AI ecosystem.

First, this strategy provides the younger generation and career-changing workers with foundational knowledge in AI and data science, delivered through flexible short and medium-term programs.

Second, both domestic and international resources are directed towards training and research facilities, while the government invests in building specialized centers at top universities.

Third, this strategy prioritizes investment in creating necessary AI tools and platforms that meet domestic needs first before targeting regional and global markets, while also encouraging the use of AI to enhance the effectiveness of research and development in other fields.

Fourth, it aims to create a favorable environment for AI businesses to grow by setting up incubation centers and attracting investment capital to nurture AI companies and brands in Vietnam.

#### Goals

- Set up **2 to 3 national AI innovation centers**
- Upgrade and establish **10 novel key AI research and training institutions**
- Have **at least 1 Vietnamese institution** ranked in **ASEAN's top 20 AI research centers**
- Increase the number of AI startups and total funding for AI field in Vietnam

#### Deadline

- 10 institutions and 2 national innovation centers: **2025**
- 3 national centers and ASEAN top 20 ranking: **2030**

#### Significance

Technology cannot advance without skilled people. This strategy addresses Vietnam's most important long-term challenge which is building a generation of AI professionals who can develop and manage AI systems effectively, especially those who can match international standards. In addition, it also recognizes that educational institutions alone are not enough and Vietnam needs a strong startup ecosystem that can turn research into real products and services to compete across regions.

---

### Strategic direction 3: Promoting the application of AI

Decision 127 describes this strategy as the bridge between AI development and real impacts and focuses on three areas.

First, it not only encourages more businesses to integrate AI into their operations to serve domestic demands, particularly in fields where data, technology, and initial funding are already available, but also supports the use of existing AI software platforms and open applications.

Second, it promotes the creation of Vietnam-made AI products to establish a national AI industry, while pushing relevant governmental parties to adopt AI-powered tools that can improve resource allocation, public administration, and urban management.

Third, it aims to improve AI-related awareness, knowledge, and qualifications of government officials, civil servants, businesses, and the public.

#### Goals

- AI is integrated into government administrative processes and public services to improve efficiency and reduce costs for citizens
- Major urban centers leverage AI to enhance operational effectiveness, such as resource allocation, social services, and city management
- Workers across different occupations develop foundational AI skills to drive productivity and innovation
- AI is deployed in national defense, public security, and emergency response situations
- AI plays a meaningful role in driving economic growth as part of Vietnam's digital transformation

#### Deadline

- AI in government services and urban management in major cities: **2025**
- AI skills among workforce, defense applications, and economic contribution: **2030**

#### Significance

This strategy is where AI investments made in infrastructure and human resources (from Strategy 1 and Strategy 2) finally produce real value for the broader economy and society. Moreover, it reflects the Vietnamese government's understanding that building AI capability is not the final goal, but the final goal here is better public services, more productive businesses, and a higher quality of life for Vietnamese citizens. The focus on raising public awareness is also important, because a national strategy can only deliver real results when people responsible for implementing it can understand and use the technology.

---

## Conclusion

The journey of Vietnam towards becoming an AI center in Southeast Asia is still ongoing, but the directions are clear. Three strategic directions are discussed in this report, including building infrastructure, developing the ecosystem, and promoting AI applications. These efforts are strongly connected, because infrastructure supports human development and both are necessary for AI to be applied effectively in reality, which will advance Vietnam's progress towards its 2030 goals.
